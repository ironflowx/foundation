# Changelog

Each release lists what it adds and changes. All published crates share one version. Foundation crates are 0.x with no semver-stability promise.

## Unreleased

- **Leases and the fence** ([#223](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/223)), breaking: a claim also takes a `running` run whose lease's deadline has been reached, a lapsed lease due at its deadline, as the next execution held by the claiming key, and counts one lost lease; the tenth in a row fails the run with `ironflowx.executor_lost` and appends `ironflowx.run.failed` in the claim's record, and the claim goes on to the next run, failing at most 100 (D97). A step's output sets the count back to 0; a lost lease spends no attempt. Every accepted call that appends renews its lease to now plus its length; a late executor whose run nobody took keeps it (D94). `Operations` gains `heartbeat` (`Heartbeat`, `executions heartbeat`), which renews 1–100 leases in one record and answers each on its own, a renewed deadline or its refusal, and `release_run` (`ReleaseRun`, `executions release`), which makes the run due now, counting nothing, and answers its due time, the same when sent again (D109); both need `execute` and meet the fence, so an implementation of `Operations` needs the two methods, and a match on `Operation` the two arms. A released execution's calls answer `Conflict`, "this execution is over", until the next claim supersedes it. `Run` gains `lost_leases` and `error` (`RunError { code, message }`) on the wire and in Rust. Execution rules gain `Command::Heartbeat` and `Release`, the entries `LeaseRenewed`, `LeaseLost`, `Released` and `RunFailed` (kinds 7 to 10; private fields, made only by `decide` with its event), `Outcome::Renewed` and `Released`, `RunError`, and `Run::lease_seconds`, `lost_leases` and `error`; `Entry::Claimed` carries `lease_seconds`; `ExecutionView::claimable` takes a limit and returns the runs in the order a claim tries them; `ExecutionTables` gains `renew`, `lose_lease`, `release` and `fail_run`, and `claim` takes the lease's length. `read.db`'s `runs` gains columns, so one written before this is rebuilt at open. A lost claim's hint now says its run waits for its lease's deadline, and the proto client checks one heartbeat result per lease. EXE-002, EXE-010, EXE-030's rows but the two failures, EXE-032, EXE-033, EXE-035, EXE-037, EXE-038 and FUN-008's rows for `Heartbeat` and `ReleaseRun` pass, and the one-execution replay check that stood in for EXE-010 is gone.
- **Definitions: every rule, replace, list** ([#220](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/220)), breaking: a put follows every rule of a function: `max_attempts` 1–100, `first_delay_seconds` 1–3,600, `max_delay_seconds` 1–86,400 and not below the first delay when both are put, each `Invalid`; at most 20 functions per event name, `ResourceExhausted` with a hint that names the event, and at most 1,000 per environment, `ResourceExhausted` (D100). An identical put still answers `changed: false`, past a cap too, and a policy field is stored only if it was put (D99). `Operations` gains `list_functions` (`ListFunctions`, `functions list`, scope `read`): functions in name order, 20 a page unless asked, at most 200, with a cursor checked as an events cursor is; an implementation of `Operations` needs the method, and a match on `Operation` the arm. The ledger gains `list_functions`, execution rules `list_functions` and `FunctionPage`, and `ExecutionView` `triggered_by`, `function_count` and `functions_after`. `ironflowx_events::Refusal` gains `resource_exhausted`. FUN-002 to FUN-007, FUN-008's row for `ListFunctions` and EXE-003 pass on the engine and through the door.
- **Tracer: functions, runs, a claim, a step, a completed run** ([#219](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/219)), breaking: a new pure crate, `ironflowx-execution` (execution rules: `Command`, `Entry`, `decide`, `fold`, `ExecutionView`, `ExecutionTables`), with its own purity allowlist, `clippy.toml` and `wasm32-unknown-unknown` check. `Operations` gains `put_function`, `get_function`, `start_run`, `get_run`, `claim_run`, `begin_step`, `complete_step` and `complete_run`, and the proto three services, `FunctionService`, `RunService` and `ExecutionService`, served by the door, called by `Client` and generated as commands; an implementation of `Operations` needs the eight methods, and a match on `Operation` the eight arms. `put_function` and `start_run` need `write`, the two reads `read`, the rest `execute`. `start_run` takes the function's name only, its run's input is JSON `null`, and it answers `duplicate: false`; `claim_run` checks `wait_seconds` and answers at once. Every call that names a lease meets the fence: `NotFound` for a lease that does not parse or names no run, `Conflict` for one that is not the run's current execution or that another key holds, each with its hint (D95). `complete_run` appends `ironflowx.run.completed` in the record that completes the run (RUN-2, D103). The ledger's `Submission` is now `Events(EventSubmission) | Execution(Command)` (events' own type is re-exported as `EventSubmission`), `Committed::outcome` an `Outcome` of either module, and it gains `get_function` and `get_run`; a record holds execution entries beside events, kinds 1 to 6 (D112), and `read.db` gains `functions`, `triggers`, `runs` and `steps`: a `read.db` written before this is rebuilt at open. `ironflowx_events::Refusal` gains `invalid`, `not_found` and `conflict`, and no constructor takes an `ErrorKind`; event rules export `check_name` and `check_value`. `ironflowx_core` gains `Lease` and the limits `MAX_FUNCTION_NAME_BYTES`, `MAX_STEP_NAME_BYTES` and `MAX_STEP_INPUT_BYTES`. `functions.feature` and `execution.feature` join the behaviors: FUN-001, FUN-008's rows for these operations, EXE-001, EXE-004, EXE-031 and EXE-039 pass on the engine and through the door, and the rest are `@pending`, run apart and failing the run if one passes. A record's execution entries read back as `ironflowx_execution::Stored`, which `fold` applies and from which no `Entry` comes out, so a decoded completion joins no decision (RUN-2). The generator makes a `oneOf` of an answer's `oneof`, and an `allOf` of them for several. Pure crates may no longer call `std::env::vars_os`, `args`, `args_os`, `temp_dir`, `home_dir`, `current_exe` or `std::process::id`, and `ci/check-deps.sh` reads a pure crate's dependencies with all its features on, for this machine's target and for wasm32.
- **The embedder's own call** ([#229](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/229)), breaking: `Engine::embedder_call(name)` gives the embedding process a `Call` admitted as admin by any engine of the same environment and incarnation (a copy of the directory included, as for a key), with no key and no secret on disk, and attributed `embedder:<name>`: its emits record it, and a key it creates names it in `created_by`. It returns an error for a name `SubmitAs::embedder` refuses. No door can carry it: the `Call` holds a grant (`Call::with_grant`, `Call::grant`) whose type is private to the engine; another engine's grant, or any other, is refused `Unauthenticated` "invalid grant". It is never revoked and never expires (D121). Breaking: `Whoami::key_id` is `Option<KeyId>`, `None` only for an embedder's call, and `WhoamiResponse.key_id` is `optional` on the wire; `KeyInfo::created_by` and `ironflowx_store::KeyRow::created_by` are `Option<Emitter>`, on the wire and in `control.db` a creating key's bare ID, as before, or `embedder:<name>`, which a 0.3 client fails to decode; `ControlPlane::insert_key` and `revoke_key` take who makes the change as `&Emitter`. What a key's call sends and receives is byte for byte unchanged. New: `Emitter::key_id`, `From<KeyId> for Emitter`, `ironflowx_authz::may_create_as_embedder`. `examples/embed` emits as itself with `embedder_call` beside its seed.
- **`Scope::Execute` and `ErrorKind::Conflict`** ([#218](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/218)), breaking: a key may hold `execute`, which no other scope implies and no operation needs yet (D107). Bootstrap, seed and recovered keys hold `read`, `write`, `admin` and `execute`. `control.db`'s format version moves to 2, and a format-1 file, as 0.2 and 0.3 wrote it, is refused at open with `Format` (D47). `ErrorKind::Conflict` (D106) is `conflict` in `ErrorInfo`, `failed_precondition` on the wire, not retryable, exit code 9; no operation answers it yet. The proto client no longer reads a bare `failed_precondition` without `ErrorInfo` as `Invalid`: it is a proxy's, `Unknown` of a call that changes state and `Unavailable` of a read (rule 2). A match on `Scope` or `ErrorKind` needs the new arm.
- **The engine's own events, and hints on refusals** ([#217](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/217)): `ironflowx_events::engine_event(EngineEvent) -> EngineEmit` makes `ironflowx.run.completed` or `ironflowx.run.failed`, with version 1, no metadata, no idempotency key and data `{"run_id","function","code"}`, plus `"error":{"code","message"}` for a failure; `EngineEmit` is opaque, and `sequence` gives engine events the next event sequences, one fresh ID each, refusing data that event rules would refuse from an emit (D118). A client's emit of either name is refused as any owned name still is. Clippy bans `engine_event` in every crate but `ironflowx-execution`, and `ci/no-engine-event.sh` backs the ban up by name in every Rust and Markdown file outside `ironflowx-events`, `ironflowx-execution` and the docs, doctests, included files and code behind an off feature included, each with a fixture (RUN-14). `ci/clippy-bans.sh` now also reads `.clippy.toml`, and no longer counts a ban that is only in a comment, whole-line or trailing. Error codes that begin with `ironflowx.` are the engine's (D111). `ironflowx_core::RunId` names a run, a UUIDv7. `Refusal` gains `hint()` and `with_hint()`, and the engine copies the hint into `OpError::hint`; no event rule gives one yet, so every answer is unchanged. Nothing appends an engine event yet.
- **Every record has its own ID** ([#216](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/216)), breaking: the ledger's log format moves to 3, and a directory written by 0.1, 0.2 or 0.3 is refused at open with `OpenError::Format` (D8). A record is now its envelope (format version, incarnation token, record ID, emitter, time), then its ordered entries, each with its kind; so far every entry is an event (D112). The record ID is a UUIDv7 the writer draws at the append, so what two copies of a directory append after the copy never shares one. `read.db`'s `meta` keeps the last applied record's ID, and open compares it with the log's record at that position instead of the newest stored event, so a read store of a diverged copy is rebuilt whatever its last record holds (D117). Open still appends nothing.
- **Size limits in one place, and `check_emit`** ([#215](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/215)): `ironflowx_core::limits`, re-exported as `ironflowx_proto::limits`, holds the size limits of what a caller sends: `MAX_EVENT_NAME_BYTES`, `MAX_DATA_BYTES`, `MAX_METADATA_BYTES`, `MAX_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_BYTES`, `MAX_BATCH_EVENTS`, `MAX_BATCH_BYTES`, `MAX_KEY_LABEL_BYTES` and `MAX_EMBEDDER_NAME_BYTES`, and the wire's `MAX_MESSAGE_BYTES` (16 MiB), which `wire::MAX_MESSAGE` now names. Event rules, credentials, attribution, the door and the proto client read them from there. `ironflowx_operations::check_emit(&EmitRequest)` runs event rules' checks of one emit without a submit, and answers as event rules answer an emit that admission let through, and `ironflowx_events::check_emit(&Emit)` is the item rules it runs, so an embedder or executor sizes a payload before sending it (D120). The values and every refusal's kind and message are unchanged. `ci/no-engine-deps.sh` now also checks `ironflowx-proto`, with every feature.
- **CI: the app ref** ([#214](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/214)): `ci/app-ref` names the `ironflowx/ironflow` branch the `conformance` job checks out, as `refs/heads/<branch>`, and reads `main`. A PR that adds an operation, a scope or an error kind, which the app must follow, sets it to the app's matching branch, so the two land as a pair; the next foundation PR sets it back to `main`. A malformed file, one with a trailing space, a CR or a BOM among them, fails the job, naming the file; `ci/purity.sh` proves each refusal with a fixture. `ci/check.sh` fetches the same ref. A PR that changes only `docs/` and Markdown still skips the suite. `ci/README.md`, "A change the app must follow", says how (D122). No crate changes.
- **Docs** ([#197](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/197)): `examples/embed` keeps access across a restart. It writes a seed from `generate_seed()` to a mode-0600 file, synced, before the first open, and passes it to every `Engine::open`, instead of taking its key from `Opened::minted`, which only a bootstrapping open returns. `architecture.md`'s Engine open says an embedder that restarts should do the same; an engine bootstrapped without a seed takes one at its next open. No behavior change.
- **Named Ironflow X**: the README, which every crate page on crates.io shows, opens on what foundation is, not as a rewrite of another engine; the keyword `ironflow` becomes `ironflowx` on each crate that has keywords; the `ironflowx` facade's description reads "Ironflow X foundation crates in one dependency.", and the crates' authors are "Ironflow X contributors".
- **Foundation builds no binary** ([#193](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/193)), breaking: `ironflowx-bin` and `ironflowx-cli` are gone, with `serve.feature`, `cli.feature`, the server crash test and the release archive pipeline. The `ironflowx` binary, its CLI and their release archives are built in [`ironflowx/ironflow`](https://github.com/ironflowx/ironflow) from 0.4; releases up to 0.3.0 stay in this repository's GitHub releases. A release here publishes crates and a tag, with notes and no assets. `ironflowx-doors` and `ironflowx-codegen` stay here and are published from 0.4.0 (D85). `events.feature` and `tenancy.feature` still run through the proto client, against a door over an engine served in the test. A `conformance` job runs `ironflowx/ironflow`'s suite against each PR, and a `wasm` job checks that `ironflowx-core`, `ironflowx-events` and `ironflowx-authz` build for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`.
- **segment-log-core 0.3.0**: the ledger's log moves from `=0.2.1` to `=0.3.0`. A writable open of a log with more than one segment now rewrites and flushes the second-newest segment's closing run, so a run found only in the page cache survives a power loss (segment-log ADR 0073); that costs one file open, one small read, one small write and one `sync_all` at each such open. A log that an older build already rolled past such a run is not repaired; after a power loss, run segment-log's repair tool. Foundation does not turn on segment-log's new `simulation` feature.
- **segment-log-core 0.2.1** ([#193](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/193)): the ledger's log moves from `=0.2.0` to `=0.2.1`, with its key-index fixes.
- **The facade holds what apps use** ([#190](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/190)), breaking: `ironflowx` re-exports only `core`, `proto`, `cell` and `operations`; `ironflowx::store`, `ironflowx::authz` and `ironflowx::events` are gone (D83). `ironflowx-cell` re-exports `Submission`, `Emit`, `Event`, `EventPage`, `ItemOutcome` and `Refusal`, so a direct ledger user reaches them as `ironflowx::cell::*`. The three crates stay published.
- **One key-expiry rule** ([#189](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/189)): `KeyRecord::expired(now)` in `ironflowx-authz` decides expiry for both admission's `recheck` and `list_keys`' status, so the two cannot disagree.
- **`Authorized` is private** ([#189](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/189)), breaking: `ironflowx_operations::Authorized` and its `grant()` are no longer exported. Nothing public took or returned one. TEN-1's compile-fail proof now shows that no other crate can name the type.
- **The ledger answers its own reads** ([#188](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/188)), breaking: `Ledger::get_event` and `Ledger::list_events` replace `Ledger::read`, which is now private, with `ReadStore` and `ironflowx_cell::StoreError`. A caller's query no longer holds the read store's lock beside the writer, and `list_events` checks a cursor with the ledger's own incarnation token. A direct caller loses `event_by_key` and `last_sequence`.
- **Ledger errors are Rust errors** ([#188](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/188)): `SubmitError`, `ReadError` and `Refusal` implement `Display` and `std::error::Error`, so `?` into `Box<dyn Error>` works. A refusal shows its message. No error implements `source()`: the inner error is in `Display`.

## 0.3.0 - Serve

The first `ironflowx` binary ([#139](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/139)).
Release archives contain the binary and MIT licence, with a separate SHA-256 checksum for each
archive. They target Linux x86-64 and AArch64 (static musl, D81), and macOS on Apple silicon.
The foundation crates and facade share version 0.3.0. The host, CLI, HTTP door and CLI generator
are built from this repository and have `publish = false`.

- **Server crash tests** ([#154](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/154)). Linux and macOS exercise the real host's restart after SIGKILL under load, during drain, and around bootstrap and recovery key publication. The release workflow check rejects builds with the private fault hooks.

- **Serve shutdown** ([#153](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/153)): SIGINT or SIGTERM stops admitting calls and drains for up to 10 seconds before closing the engine. A signal during open lets it finish and publish a minted key. A second signal exits 1 immediately. A ledger fault before close exits 8 even without traffic. A control-plane failure still allows serving. The dev and release binaries abort on panic (D63, D72).

- **Docs** ([#134](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/134)): corrects 0.2.0's #97 entry and `Engine::open`'s page. A bootstrapping open that fails before its ledger is in place tries to undo itself; if the undo fails, a bootstrap it did not delete stays unmarked, for a later open to finish. If an open's mark commits but it does not return, no later open reports its lost secret. No behavior change.
- **Docs** ([#135](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/135)): qualifies 0.2.0's #93, #94, #96, #97 and #98 entries. A control-plane commit that SQLite fails after writing it to the WAL, such as at a failed fsync, is unsettled: the next open of `control.db` may find it landed, with or without a crash (D48). An open that failed may so have finished its bootstrap, losing a minted secret, or have rotated its seed, against its answer, and a `create_key`, `revoke_key`, `recover_admin_key` or recorded expiry that failed may have taken effect. A failed creation or recovery can therefore leave a key in `control.db` whose secret the call never returned. `architecture.md` says what a host does about each. No behavior change.
- **Docs**: a review of the docs against the code. `ErrorKind::Unavailable`'s page no longer says that only the ledger answers it, until reopened: a control-plane failure can be retried at once, unless a call has panicked under its lock. `Engine`'s pages list every error kind, what a drop without `close` does and the need for tokio, and `Clock`'s says that `now` must not panic. `architecture.md` adds what a cancelled key change can still do, what a backup is, file modes, and `Ledger::open` on an engine's root. No behavior change.
- **Secret checks** ([#131](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/131)): the check that no error type holds a `Secret` covers a generic error used with one; the search for stored or logged secrets covers a refused `recover_admin_key` and escaped bytes; with `__fault-points`, the key hook is told a recovered key and its secret (TEN-3's stated exception). CI checks the error code each `compile_fail` doctest names. No behavior change without `__fault-points`.
- **Error kinds and operations** ([#141](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/141)): `ErrorKind::retryable()` is true for `Unavailable` only: `Unknown` and `ResourceExhausted` are no longer retryable, and their hints still say what to do (D70, which supersedes that half of D44). It promises no second event, not no second key. `ErrorKind` gains `Internal` (D69), which breaks an exhaustive match on it, and `exit_code()`. `Operation` gains `ALL`, `rpc()` and `changes_state()`.
- **A page of events** ([#142](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/142)): `list_events` stops a page before its data and metadata pass 3 MiB, with at least one event if any is left, and `next` continues from there; until now only the count limited a page, so do not take one shorter than its limit for the last. The read store loads no event past the page. `EventsView::events_before` takes a `PageBudget` in place of a count, which breaks an implementation or a call of it: only `list_events` makes one, and it says, by its size, whether to load each event (D73).
- **Engine health** ([#143](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/143)): a host asks an open engine how it is with `Engine::health`, which reports the control plane and the ledger each alone, and learns of a ledger fault with no traffic from `Engine::faulted`, a future that resolves once, when the ledger becomes `Unavailable`; a close in which nothing fails leaves it pending. Neither takes a key, and neither is an operation (TEN-1). Both are in `ironflowx-operations`, with `Health` and `ControlPlaneState`; `Ledger` gains `state` and `faulted`, with `LedgerState` and `Fault`. A read whose caller gave up now fails the ledger as any other does, if its query panics or the read store fails; until now a later call found out, or, of a read-store failure that had cleared, none. `Ledger::close` on a runtime that has shut down, which panics, now waits for the writer before it releases the directory.
- **Serve** ([#144](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/144)): `ironflowx serve` opens the engine in a data directory, writes a minted key to `admin.key`, and answers `whoami` over Connect, gRPC and gRPC-Web, with `/health` and `/ready`. Two new crates, both unpublished: `ironflowx-doors`, the HTTP door, and `ironflowx-bin`, the binary. `ironflowx-proto` gains its `.proto` files and three features, none on by default: `client`, the proto client (`Client`, `InvalidUrl`), which carries `whoami` and answers `Internal` for every other operation; `wire`, the generated messages and their mapping; and `server`, the generated service traits. With none on, its dependencies do not change. D50, D51, D53, D55, D59, D62, D75–D78.
- **CLI** ([#145](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/145)): `ironflowx whoami` asks an engine who a key is, and `ironflowx version` says what the binary is. A command is generated from the proto at build time, and a method without a command, a summary or a valid example fails the build. An answer is JSON on stdout, with every field that is not `optional`, in one line or, on a terminal, indented; a failure is one envelope on stderr and its kind's exit code. The key comes from `--key-file` or `IRONFLOWX_KEY`, never from `admin.key` unasked; the engine from `--url` or `IRONFLOWX_URL`; a call takes at most 30 s. A usage error, `serve`'s too, is an envelope and exit 2, where `serve`'s was text, and `ironflowx` alone, which printed help, is one. Two new crates, both unpublished: `ironflowx-cli` and `ironflowx-codegen`, the generator. `ironflowx-proto`'s `Client` gains `with_timeout`, its `wire` feature `FILE_DESCRIPTOR_SET`, and its proto the custom options `cli`, `summary`, `example` and `json_text`. D52, D61, D64, D65.
- **Keys and environments over the wire** ([#146](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/146)): `create_key`, `list_keys`, `revoke_key` and `list_environments` are served over Connect, gRPC and gRPC-Web, `Client` carries them, and each is a command: `keys create`, `keys list`, `keys revoke`, `environments list`. Scopes travel as strings (D71). A request the wire cannot carry (a limit of 0, an unknown scope, a time finer than a millisecond or outside years 0001–9999) is `Invalid` before the key is read, and `Client` refuses such an expiry before it sends. Where no engine answered, `Client` answers `Unknown` for `create_key` and `revoke_key`, and `Unavailable` for a read as before. The hint of a `create_key` that answers `Unavailable` as it stores its key no longer says to retry at once, in process too, but to list the keys before the retry, and again after the engine reopens. The `CreateKey` answer is the one wire message that holds a key, which its `Debug` does not print (TEN-3).
- **Events over the wire** ([#147](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/147)): `emit`, `emit_batch`, `get_event` and `list_events` are served over Connect, gRPC and gRPC-Web, so every operation is, and `Client` carries them all: none is `Internal` from it any more. Each is a command: `events emit`, `events emit-batch`, `events get`, `events list`. Data and metadata are JSON text in a string field, stored and returned byte for byte (D54); text that is not UTF-8 is `Invalid` before the key is read, and `Client` refuses such data or metadata before it sends, a batch whole (D74). Where no engine answered, `Client` answers `Unknown` for `emit` and `emit_batch`, a batch answered with another number of results than it has items, and an emit over 16 MiB; its message for the four names the Connect code alone, or that the answer does not decode, since a body can hold event data. Branch on `next`: a page shorter than its limit is not necessarily the last. **A Connect JSON client with a 4 MiB receive limit raises it**, to 16 MiB: a page can be nearly 6.5 MiB in JSON, where escaping can double it, and stays under 4 MiB in binary, on gRPC and on gRPC-Web. D49.
- **What the client knows, and `https`** ([#148](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/148)): where no engine answered, `Client` says what may have happened. A call whose connection never opened is `Unavailable`, a state change too, where it was `Unknown`; so a retry of it is safe. A state change that may have run stays `Unknown`, but for an answer the client cannot tell from the engine's (`architecture.md`, Wire, known limits). A code of the server's library, sent as a Connect server sends it, with no detail, is its own kind: `invalid_argument`, `out_of_range` and `failed_precondition` are `Invalid`, `resource_exhausted` is `ResourceExhausted`, and `unimplemented` is `Internal`, with the hint that the engine is older than the client. A request over 16 MiB is `ResourceExhausted` and not sent, where a write was `Unknown` and a read `Unavailable`. The message of a failure that is not the engine's, of a call that was sent, names the HTTP status, the Connect code, or that the answer does not decode, and nothing of the body, where `whoami`'s, `list_environments`', `list_keys`' and `revoke_key`'s held the body's text. The hint of an `Unknown` emit fits the request, the engine's as the client's own, which had none: an `emit` with an idempotency key keeps the engine's; one without says to check first what landed, and an `emit_batch` to retry unchanged only if every item had a key and keeps it. `ironflowx-proto` gains the feature `client-tls`, off by default, which adds `https` URLs to `Client`, with roots from the system's store; without it they are `InvalidUrl`, as before. The CLI takes `https` URLs. D56.
- **Every operation from the CLI** ([#149](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/149)): a command takes its request from flags, one per scalar field (`ironflowx events emit --name order.placed --data '{"id":1}'`), or whole from `--json`, as text, `@<file>` or `-`, stdin; JSON text is sent byte for byte, from a flag or `@<file>`. `--json` refuses a field the request does not have, at any depth, where the door ignores it, so a mistyped `idempotency_key` stores no event (D79); a JSON value in place of JSON text; and an array for a message. Event data and metadata are printed as the JSON they hold, with only the whitespace between tokens removed, where they were a string of it (D65). `keys create` refuses a label that repeats the caller's own key, since its answer, the new key in it, would not be printed; `events get` and `events list` still refuse to print an event that holds it (TEN-3).
- **`describe`** ([#150](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/150)): the CLI lists every operation with its scope, summary and whether it changes state, together with the exit codes. Ask about one operation for its wire request and printed answer schemas, and a request example. Both commands work without a key or a running engine. D67, D82.

- **Host through the engine** ([#174](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/174)): `ironflowx-operations` exports `is_bootstrapped`, a blocking read of the bootstrap mark that creates no directory or database, and re-exports `HostClock`, `OpenError` and `StoreError`. The binary uses the engine to check whether a bootstrap is marked before it accepts an existing `admin.key`. The binary drops its direct normal dependencies on the ledger and control plane crates, and CI checks that boundary.
- **Proto client through the facade** ([#175](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/175)): `ironflowx` gains opt-in `client` and `client-tls` features. An embedder can use `ironflowx::proto::Client` as its `Operations` adapter over HTTP, or over HTTPS with `client-tls`. Both features are off by default.
- **Host startup** ([#151](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/151)): `serve` reads flags and environment settings, accepts a seed, and probes writes and hard links before it can mint. Refusals exit with the corresponding kind and end stderr with the error envelope. Call logs carry the operation, outcome, duration and any validly formatted presented key's ID. `seed generate` makes a key and prints it with its ID. D57, D58, D60, D68, D80.
- **Admin-key recovery from the CLI** ([#152](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/152)): `ironflowx recover-key` works with or without a running engine. It checks and probes the file destination before recovering a key to `admin.key`. Use `--stdout` to receive the key and its ID as JSON. A delivery failure names the key ID and says how to revoke it. D60.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-11-24

The second release, 0.2 Tenancy ([#64](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/64)): every call carries a scoped key, and the engine knows who exists. It adds three crates, `ironflowx-operations`, `ironflowx-authz` and `ironflowx-store`, and the facade re-exports them as `operations`, `authz` and `store`. [`examples/embed`](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/blob/v0.2.0/examples/embed/src/main.rs) bootstraps an engine, emits an event, reads it back with a `read` key and sees that key's emit refused. A 0.1 ledger directory is refused, not migrated (D39).

- **Admin-key recovery** ([#98](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/98)): `recover_admin_key` stores a fresh admin key in a data directory and returns its secret, once: all three scopes, no expiry, origin `Recovered`, labelled `recovered`. It is a host's function, not an operation: reaching the directory is the authority. It blocks, takes no engine lock and works beside a running engine, whose next call accepts the key. On a directory never bootstrapped, or whose bootstrap its transaction finds unmarked, it answers `RecoverError::NotReady` and stores no key; a `control.db` of another format, or a failure to read or write it, is `RecoverError::Store`. Revoking the last admin key needs no guard (D34).
- **Open safety** ([#97](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/97)): every refused or failed open has a defined outcome, and the engine never deletes a ledger (D32, D40, D41). `EngineError` gains `NotAnEngineRoot` (a bare ledger's directory), `Format` (a `control.db` of another format), `Orphaned` (the control plane was lost) and `LedgerMissing` (the ledger was lost). A bootstrapping open that fails before its ledger is in place undoes itself. An open that finds a committed but unmarked bootstrap and succeeds finishes it: a minted key, whose secret no open returned, is revoked and `Opened::lost_secret` set, and, without a seed, a replacement is returned. A seeded bootstrap has no such key. The ledger gains `open_blocking` and `open_existing_blocking`, which answers the new `OpenError::Missing` instead of creating a ledger.
- **Everything runs through the engine** ([#95](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/95)): `LocalOperations`, `ironflowx-cell`'s `Operations` adapter over a bare ledger, is removed, and `Engine` implements `Operations`, which gains `whoami` and `list_environments`, so `Operations` always means credentialed (D36). Import the trait to call the engine's operations. An embedder without keys calls `Ledger::submit` and `read`. A failed ledger's hint says to reopen the engine. The events scenarios, the crash test (D23) and `examples/embed` run through an engine.
- **Seeds** ([#96](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/96)): `Engine::open` takes an optional seed, a key the host supplies to be the bootstrap key, and then mints none. On a later open, a seed with a new key ID revokes the current seed and takes its place, leaving other keys alone; a revoked seed opens and stays revoked. `Opened::seed` reports `SeedState::Current` or `Revoked`. A malformed seed is `EngineError::MalformedSeed`, before anything is read; one whose key ID is already another key's is `SeedConflict`, and no key changes (D33). Each write of an open holds the root's lock until it ends, even when the open is cancelled.
- **Expiry sticks, and the key cap** ([#94](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/94)): a key refused as expired has its expiry recorded, and from then on is never accepted again, whatever the clock does later, across restarts too. If recording fails, the refusal is the same, but a clock set back can accept the key until a later refusal records it (D42). At most 1,000 created keys per environment hold a slot, until each is revoked or its expiry is recorded; at the cap `create_key` answers the new `ErrorKind::ResourceExhausted`, retryable after a revoke (D44).
- **List and revoke keys** ([#93](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/93)): `list_keys` pages newest first, 100 keys by default and 1,000 at most, with each key's label, scopes, origin, creator, times and status, never a secret. `revoke_key` keeps the row, and the key is refused from its next call (D35). A key change whose calling key was revoked or expired meanwhile is `Unauthenticated` and changes no key, except that a calling key that only expired has its expiry recorded (D42, D43). A failed control-plane read or write is `Unavailable`, retryable at once, except a failure to record an expiry (D44); the engine stays open, and key operations work while the ledger is `Unavailable`.
- **Scoped keys** ([#92](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/92)): `create_key` takes scopes (`read` unless asked otherwise), an optional expiry and an optional label, and returns the secret once, in a `CreatedKey`, whose `KeyInfo` says what the key is, its `KeyOrigin` (in core) included. A key holds no scope its creator lacks and expires no later than its creator (D27, D28). Every operation's scope comes from one exhaustive `required_scope` (D37); without it a call is the new `ErrorKind::PermissionDenied`, and its hint names the scope (D31). A key is refused from its expiry on, by the ledger's clock.
- **Bad keys** ([#108](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/108)): no key, or an empty one, is `Unauthenticated` "authentication required". A malformed key is "invalid API key", with a hint naming the key format. An unknown key, a wrong secret and another engine's key get one identical answer with no hint, as a revoked or expired key does; a key of another environment or incarnation is refused by both the lookup and `verify` (D29, D30).
- **Emit and read with a key** ([#107](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/107)): the engine's `emit`, `emit_batch`, `get_event` and `list_events` admit the key on every call, with no cache. Each event records the key that emitted it, as `key:<key-id>`, taken from the verified grant; a lint confines `Grant::submit_as` to the engine (D36).
- **Fixed: secrets in freed memory** ([#124](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/pull/124)): parsing and minting a key leave no copy of its secret in freed heap memory (TEN-3). Which keys are accepted does not change.
- **Engine tracer** ([#91](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/91)): three new crates. `ironflowx-operations` holds the engine. `Engine::open` locks the root, bootstraps a fresh data directory in one control-plane transaction, builds the ledger in staging and renames it into place, and returns the bootstrap key's secret once. `ironflowx-authz` holds the credentials: the `ixk_` key format, its SHA-256 `KeyDigest` compared in constant time, and `verify`. `ironflowx-store` is the control plane, `control.db`. A call carrying the key is admitted (parse, look up, verify) and can ask `whoami` and `list_environments`. Core gains `TenantId`, `ProjectId`, `Scope`, `Address`, `Secret` (zeroed on drop, not printable or cloneable), `ErrorKind::Unauthenticated` and `retryable()`. `Call::with_key` arrives and `Call` loses `Clone`; `OpError` gains `hint`. The behaviors runner runs every feature file and checks each file's count; `tenancy.feature` lands with ACC-001, 002, 003 and 009 running. D38, D45 and D46.
- **Attribution** ([#90](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/90)): every event records who emitted it. Core gains `KeyId`, `Emitter` and `SubmitAs`; `Ledger::submit` takes a `SubmitAs` beside the unchanged `Submission`, `ironflowx_events::fold` takes the record's `Emitter`, and both event types gain `emitted_by` (D36). The ledger format moves to 2, so a 0.1 directory answers `Format` (D39). A lint confines `SubmitAs::key` to the credentials crate.
- **README and crate pages** ([#63](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/pull/63), [#102](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/102)): the README states the purpose and what 0.2 is, and shows the architecture with the crate and emit-then-read diagrams. Its links and images are absolute, so they work on crates.io; 0.1.0's example link there is broken. The emit-then-read diagram shows the committed head and applied position as `p + 1`, as the code does. `ironflowx-core`'s and `ironflowx-proto`'s pages name what they gain.
- **Ledger** ([#65](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/pull/65)): at most 16 submissions wait for the writer; more callers wait for a slot (D24). `open` takes a new `<dir>/ledger.lock` (D25), so a sealed log, whose own lock is released, still answers `Locked` to a second opener. A read store whose newest event disagrees with the log is rebuilt. With an empty log, a read store whose applied position cannot be read answers `Io` and is kept, and one with recorded progress answers `Corrupt` before genesis is written. `open` syncs the parent of each directory it creates. If the writer dies, a submission queued or being decided answers `Unavailable`, not `Unknown`; only one whose append began answers `Unknown`.
- **Docs:** the C3 and restart diagrams no longer show a pending view or a rollback, and the restart diagram writes the applied position as one past the record. The `Corrupt` runbook adds the case where the ledger refuses a record, and narrows when segment-log's `repair truncate` refuses a class F plan. - **Crash test** ([#99](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/99)): kills inside a bootstrap, its undo, a finishing open and a seed rotation, each with one defined outcome (TEN-9, TEN-11, TEN-12); the child opens with the parent's seed, and tells it each key it is about to store over a socket of their own (D23). - **Tenancy close-out** ([#101](https://github.com/ironflowx/foundation/issues/101)): a test searches an engine's data directory, while it runs and after close, and all its process prints, and finds no secret (TEN-3). `control.db`'s format is 1, its schema pinned by a test (D47).
